# Player NPC Performance Diagnostics And Budgets

`PlayerNpcPerformanceMonitor` owns the rolling 100-tick TPS/MSPT inspector text and scoped slow-tick warnings. It intentionally does not mirror Minecraft's outer-loop `Can't keep up` warning or maintain accumulated schedule-debt snapshots. Keep those systems separate.

TPS trace lines are correlation snapshots of active Player NPC states, not proof that the named goal consumed the whole server tick. Fix expensive code paths rather than hiding states or raising thresholds.

Current resource-search boundaries:

- `PlayerNpcAiWorkBudget` is the server-thread admission gate shared by expensive Player NPC search/path batches. It resets from the absolute server tick and permits one batch across all dimensions each server tick. Individual batches must therefore remain small; the gate prevents two NPC path batches from coinciding without starving an eleven-NPC herd at the former ten-slices-per-second limit. It complements rather than replaces each goal's randomized cadence. Admission denial means retry later; it is not a terminal search failure.
- `TreeAi.findNearest(...)` observes loaded chunks only and has hard per-call nearest-first log-search and leaf-inspection slices. Recursive connected-log and leaf reads must retain `hasChunkAt` guards. A miss is allowed to make partial progress because exploration moves the search center and GatherLogs caches discoveries.
- `GatherLogsGoal` guards candidate columns before height reads, reuses a cached tree/log queue between its nearby predicate and activation, and shares one mutable navigation-path budget across the complete nearby/target-selection pass. Never reset the stand-path budget per log candidate. Queue rebuilds, post-break/invalid-target reselection, and pillar dirt/base/descent recovery from a running gather route are expensive batches too: they must reacquire server admission because they bypass `canUse()`. A denied running reselection remains PENDING inside the active goal rather than stopping, cooling down, or path-testing the remaining queue anyway. Pillar dirt discovery keeps a nearest-first column cursor, scans at most 96 columns per admitted pass, permits one total stand path across all dirt candidates in that slice, and reuses the successful path when movement begins. Explore defers its own route selection for one tick after a failed one-path log proximity probe, so those two paths cannot share an admitted tick. A partial pass is PENDING, not "no dirt."
- `StoneAi` has hard per-slice total inspected-position, cluster-count, and cluster-size limits; nearest-first partial discovery is valid because throttled retries and gathering/digging/exploration advance discovery from the current center. A failed `GatherStoneGoal` activation or nearby/single-stand probe may build at most one nearest stand path; measured 200+ ms idle-ending mining activations show why a single admitted batch also needs a strict local bound. `GatherStoneGoal` caches nearby-target predicates briefly, shares one navigation-path budget across the whole nested target-selection pass, and acquires shared admission before its StoneAi/path batch; do not reset a path budget inside each candidate. Unsafe-stand recovery selects only the enclosing home, farm, or local egress region, never all three, and separates its one selection path from the movement path onto a later admitted tick. `DigDownForStoneGoal` acquires the same admission before nearby-stone/site work and periodic higher-priority stone/ore rechecks, caches expensive continuation eligibility, skips unloaded dig-site columns, and caps site path attempts at two per admitted activation.
- Successful stone continuation does not format failure diagnostics or query blueprint missing-material state. That diagnostic is built only on the stopping branch; otherwise its 20-tick cadence can defeat the cheap cobblestone-demand short circuit and repeatedly walk the complete build layout. A successful block destruction also yields after queuing connected candidates, so synchronous drops/lighting/neighbors are not compounded with StoneAi/path reselection until the next admitted tick.
- Mining ore/cave discovery is subject to the same rule. `ExploreCaveOreGoal` admits activation before its cursor-sliced ore scan and stand path; one pass scans at most 512 offsets and creates at most one selection path. An incomplete volume pass is PENDING and retries with jitter instead of applying the full failed-search cooldown; only a completed miss backs off. Its running obstruction/repath and post-break cluster selection branches reacquire admission, and cluster stand selection shares the same one-path cap. `MiningCaveStrollGoal` admits both activation discovery and cadence repaths and probes at most one candidate path per scan, while running `DigDownForStoneGoal` admits its periodic long-cave probe. Denial defers with jitter and does not count as a failed cave search.
- `ExploreAroundGoal` participates in shared admission for activation, periodic continuation predicates, and reached-target chaining. Each selection/fallback slice creates at most one navigation path, reuses the successful path, requires a complete path for distant destinations, and treats safe-drop as a genuinely local descent only. A wet NPC runs local survival-first `WaterEscapeAi` without selecting a distant pathless work target; a failed local escape backs off for several seconds. Pathless or stalled far targets are retired with randomized backoff instead of being retained for the full exploration timeout. Its Building/log local-surface fallback must retain a hard per-search column budget rather than sweeping the full radius. Route-foliage memory may copy the short path-node window only when the path object or next-node index changes, not once per moving explorer per tick.
- `PickupNearbyItemGoal` sorts candidates first, inspects only a bounded prefix, shares one total selection path budget, and reuses the chosen path. Running pickup navigation does not repath while a live path is still active, running retarget goes back through admitted `canUse()`, and recovery direction paths have a small hard cap. All new path batches participate in shared admission. After recovery is exhausted, quarantine that exact item briefly so a persistent unreachable drop cannot immediately capture the NPC again.
- `PlayerNpcEntity` idle resource escape caches its bounded surface search for roughly the full stuck-watch interval, resets that cache only after meaningful movement, and participates in shared admission. It must not sweep height columns every idle tick, and every height query must first prove the column is loaded. Passive contact-item pickup queries are staggered across four ticks; explicit pickup-goal contact remains immediate.
- Bootstrap/base searches are incremental: `FarmAi` persists PENDING/EXHAUSTED cursors across bounded site-search passes, and `ManageHomeBaseGoal` persists a non-builder chest candidate cursor with one total path budget and a longer negative-result backoff. `FarmSetupGoal` also owns one admitted activation-wide path budget; repair-dirt discovery uses a loaded-column cursor and must not reset a stand-path allowance for every dirt candidate. Successful interaction/approach paths are retained for goal start, and the initial move owns the repath interval rather than recreating the route on the next tick. Running invalid-stand recovery reacquires admission. While a farm or repair-dirt pass is PENDING, do not report terminal exhaustion. While the initial farm-site pass is PENDING, farm-area exploration stays suppressed; only a fully exhausted local search unlocks roaming to a new search center.
- `PlayerNpcGoalTraceLogger` caps trace serialization/logging per server tick, skips unloaded detail blocks, and uses `PlayerNpcForceTickManager`'s tracked registry while force management is enabled instead of enumerating every entity in every level each tick. `trace all` means all current scheduler-resource holders, not all loaded NPCs: it rotates only active workers/current probes/current expensive-slice consumers, removes snapshots when ownership ends, discovers holder state transitions on that rotating cadence, and rate-limits progress-only changing snapshots to ten seconds. Waiting/strolling NPCs are intentionally excluded. `/smart_npc resources` reports the effective worker limit, active/probe/expensive ownership, queue count, and bounded per-holder state without enabling trace output.
- Force management uses one moving distance-2 ticket anchor per NPC. Do not restore the old 3x3 grid of force-tick anchors: ticket propagation already provides the loaded fringe, while independent neighbor anchors multiply ticking chunks per distant NPC. The level-31 center keeps the NPC entity ticking, but the Forge-only `forceTicks` flag stays false. Setting it true additionally runs distant center chunks through natural spawning and `tickChunk` random ticks as if a real player were nearby. Scheduled ticks and ticking block entities still use the level-31/32 status, while crop/random-tick growth does not run solely because of the NPC ticket, matching vanilla `/forceload`. Refresh skips unchanged ticket sets and persistence, checks profile/display metadata on a slower stagger, and does not reconcile the whole world again for every joining viewer. Join tab initialization is one batch.
- Terraform actionability is cached for a staggered one-second window because many builder predicates query the same blueprint preparation state. Actual target selection and any running-goal reselection still acquire `PlayerNpcAiWorkBudget`; a deferred rescan releases the current target and retries rather than overlapping another NPC's expensive batch. Clear-target discovery resumes across 64 blueprint blocks per admitted slice, then support discovery resumes across 16 footprint columns per slice; completing the clear phase never chains the support pass into the same server tick.
- Terraform preparation predicates never refresh the footprint cache themselves. The admitted Terraform activation publishes `hasPrep`, actionable-now, and shovel-needed together from its single target selection; BuildHouse, resource arbitration, home/sleep checks, crafting, and diagnostics consume that result. After a valid home/layout exists, an initial or expired cache miss conservatively keeps preparation pending until Terraform receives admission, preventing both premature building and an unbudgeted selector scan. Before first-home selection, Terraform stays ineligible so it cannot suppress required supply gathering or steal BuildHouse's bootstrap admission.
- Finished-home checks follow the same rule even when the selected daily job is not Building. `isHomeLayoutFinished` may conservatively wait for a future admitted Terraform refresh, but `BeingAtHome` and finished-home exploration must never invoke a strict full-footprint `findNextTarget` from an idle selector tick.
- Existing-home BuildHouse eligibility checks Terraform's published preparation result first, then consumes the shared home-work cache; it does not repeat unfinished-placement, material, and footprint passes after Terraform has already retained preparation priority. Home-work and missing-material refreshes resume across at most 8 blueprint blocks per admitted slice; priority probes reuse the same-context stale result when another NPC owns the current slice. Return-home predicates do not begin this scan when the NPC is already in the home work area or its ordinary return cooldown is active.
- `BeingAtHomeGoal` never path-tests every utility/interior candidate during AFK selection. It validates loaded stand geometry, chooses one candidate, and lets the normal movement/stuck-recovery path handle that selected position.
- `PathNavigationAi.moveToWithLocalFallback` supports a caller-owned local-path limit. Terraform permits one local fallback after its direct path (two path creations maximum per movement batch), not the generic sixteen-candidate fallback. A failed dense-terrain route is retried on later admitted cadences or handed to bounded obstruction clearing rather than multiplying one server tick.
- `ResourceAi` shares one logs/stone/dirt inventory snapshot per NPC tick instead of rescanning every slot for every goal predicate and diagnostic. The Player NPC container listener invalidates the snapshot immediately on mutations, and cached main/offhand category/count signatures detect equipment swaps that bypass the container listener. GatherLogs, GatherStone, and Pickup publish state/target transitions immediately but rate-limit progress-only detail formatting and synced metadata to a five-tick cadence. Block-breaking crack packets are sent only when the target or visible 0-9 stage changes, not for every mining tick.

`PlayerNpcAiWorkBudget` is a server-thread CPU admission scheduler, not a percentage of allocated memory. The routine-work wrapper gives a queued NPC one tick to evaluate its job predicates; only a delegate that actually starts occupies a worker slot, so a prerequisite-blocked NPC cannot reserve the window. A queued probe request that stops refreshing expires after five ticks, preventing an NPC diverted into unwrapped combat/safety behavior from blocking the queue head. A running delegate releases its slot on stop, yields promptly if the automatic/fixed limit shrinks, and is cleanly time-sliced after two minutes when another NPC is waiting. Combat, targeting, healing, fleeing, and emergency movement are not wrapped. Within scheduled workers/probes, one bounded expensive search/path batch per server tick is distributed by NPC UUID in request-order round robin; every call site supplies its owning NPC so fixed entity tick order cannot repeatedly win an anonymous global permit.

The `aiScheduler.processingNpcLimit` config is a worker count. `-1` selects an automatic one-to-four capability ceiling from processor count and maximum heap, stays at one worker until at least 20 valid MSPT samples exist, then adjusts from rolling server MSPT: with the automatic 40 ms target, at most four workers require <=32 ms, at most three require <=40 ms, at most two require <=45 ms, and anything slower resolves to one. `aiScheduler.targetServerMspt=-1` selects that 40 ms target to leave headroom below the 50 ms 20-TPS deadline. RAM is only a conservative ceiling because searches and path creation execute serially on the server thread; the live MSPT feedback is authoritative. A non-worker denied a routine turn may use `AiBudgetWaitingStrollGoal`, whose two-block local fallback uses `MoveControl` and loaded stand-state checks without creating a navigation path.
